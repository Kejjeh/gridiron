# RB-13: touchdown-driven outcome spread, running backs (pre-registered 2026-11-02)

**Status: PRE-REGISTERED.** Written before any score was seen. The only runs
before this were `--coverage-only` (coverage and the leak check, no score)
and a plumbing test of the harness in which every real outcome was replaced
by a random draw before anything was fitted, so no real score exists.

## Idea
Roadmap RB-13, "TD-driven outcome spread" (kind D, workstream D:
distributions instead of single numbers). The page gives every running back
one normal: the served mean (advanced_v1, or the stack where Sleeper's number
is in) and the baseline's sd, max(0.55 × baseline, 1.5)
(`gridiron.projection.WEEKLY_CV`, a literature value, not a fit). That normal
is the ± the page prints and what its P(win) sums. The shipped set (expected
points, shares, depth chart) already carries the MEAN; every running-back
feature block scored so far (RB-02 to RB-11, ten of them) failed against it.
This block does not touch the mean. It changes the shape: a running back's
week is six-point lumps on top of a yardage total.

## Hypothesis
Two backs with the same mean are not spread alike. The one whose expected
points come from touchdowns (the goal-line role) has more of his week in
six-point lumps, so a Poisson count of touchdowns plus a right-skewed
yardage part describes his outcomes better than one symmetric normal. The
touchdown share is volume (expected touchdowns price his opportunities by
field position), so it updates fast; no efficiency enters (rule #6).

## Distribution inputs (`COLUMNS`)
| column | position | definition, as of the weeks before the row's week | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `td_share_std` | RB | Σ expected touchdown points ÷ Σ expected fantasy points over his games this season. Each game's expected stats (`rush_touchdown_exp`, `rec_touchdown_exp`, `rush_yards_gained_exp`, `rec_yards_gained_exp`, `receptions_exp`, the two-point `_exp` columns) are scored with the league's weights, read from `gridiron.scoring.fantasy_points` through unit stat lines (rule #2) | nflverse `ff_opportunity` (weekly), regular-season games only |
| `td_games_std` | RB | how many such games | same |
| `td_share_prior` | RB | his last-season share, shrunk to the position's league share last season with weight g / (g + 3), g = his games last season | same, season − 1 |

Missing when: no earlier game this season (`td_share_std`, `td_games_std`).
The distribution then uses the prior alone (weight 0 on the season to date).
The prior is never missing. These are distribution inputs, not ridge
features, so no `__known` indicator enters anything.

## Distribution (`pmf`, `fit`; shared code `blocks/_p7_distributions.py`)
For a row with the page's mean μ:
- share s = w · `td_share_std` + (1 − w) · `td_share_prior`, w = g / (g + 3),
  g = `td_games_std` (w = 0 when missing), clipped to [0, 0.95];
- touchdowns N ~ Poisson(s · μ / 6), 6 being the league's rushing and
  receiving touchdown value (asserted equal);
- the rest (yards, catches, two-point tries, fumbles) Y ~ gamma with mean
  (1 − s) · μ and sd a + b · (1 − s) · μ;
- points = 6N + Y on the 0.1-point lattice (this league's points are
  multiples of 0.1), 0 to 90 points, the tail folded into the last bin, then
  moved so the mean is exactly μ with every point still on the 0.1 grid: by
  whole tenths, and the remaining fraction of a tenth as a one-tenth
  Bernoulli step (`on_grid_mean`; for an outcome on the grid this is never
  worse in CRPS than sliding the support off it). The move is under 0.05
  points. The harness refuses a run in which any row's mean differs from
  the page's by more than 1e-6.
- Known limits, kept on purpose: the lattice starts at 0, so a negative week
  (a lost fumble, negative yards: 1.1% of the RB rows) always sits below the
  distribution; touchdowns and yardage are independent given μ.
- (a, b): one pair per fold, fitted on the six training seasons by minimum
  mean CRPS (Nelder-Mead from (1.0, 0.4), a in [0.05, 8], b in [0, 1.5]).
- μ is the harness's: the shipped ridge refit with the row's season held
  out; the training rows' μ come from models fit on the five seasons other
  than the test season and their own, so (a, b) are fitted on
  out-of-sample means like the ones they are scored on.

## Test
- Position: RB.
- Harness: `scripts/research/distribution_backtest.py` (block_runner.py's
  tables, rows and folds; pwin_calibration.py's leagues; neither changed).
- Rows: block_runner.py's RB rows (weeks 4–18, players Sleeper projected),
  2019–2025, seven season folds, every other season training.
- Comparator: the page's normal, N(μ, max(0.55 × baseline, 1.5)).
- Bar: a lower mean CRPS than the page's normal in every one of the seven
  folds, AND the pooled P(win) Brier over the seven folds' synthetic leagues
  not worse (≤), both decided on unrounded numbers. Leagues:
  pwin_calibration.py's 12-team leagues (its dealing,
  seeds and slot pools, checked against its own `week_matchups`), dealt
  from the fold's rows and means, weeks 4–17, 32 seeded leagues a week. Every
  starter keeps the page's normal except the running backs (both RB slots
  and any back dealt to FLEX), whose distributions are this one; P(win) is
  computed exactly on the lattice.
- Reported, never the bar: pinball loss at 10/50/90%, the share of actuals
  inside the 10–90% interval, log loss, a week-clustered bootstrap of the
  Brier change, and a fitted normal N(μ, a + bμ) fitted the same way on the
  same rows (is a gain the shape, or only a refitted spread?).
- Coverage seen before registering (no scores):
  - `td_share_std`: 2019 96%, 2020 96%, 2021 97%, 2022 96%, 2023 97%, 2024 95%, 2025 96%
  - `td_games_std`: the same
  - `td_share_prior`: 100% every season

**PASS** on the pre-registered bar. CRPS is lower than the page's normal
in all 7 folds, by 0.160 to 0.183 points (mean -0.172; pooled 2.920
against 3.092, 5.6% lower). The pooled P(win) Brier is not worse:
0.238085 against 0.238118 over 18,811 matchups, -0.0000334 unrounded
(week-clustered 95% interval -0.0001333 to +0.0000616; 76% of the
bootstrap draws not worse). It is lower in six folds and higher in 2024.

Most of the gain is the shape, not only a refitted spread: RB-13 is below
the fitted normal in every fold (pooled 3.018), and beats the page on
pinball loss at 10%, 50% and 90% in every fold (pooled 0.630 / 2.032 /
1.238 against 0.732 / 2.124 / 1.305). As for tight ends, the median is
where the normal cannot follow (its median is its mean; a back's week is
skewed). The page's spread is too narrow for running backs: its 10-90%
interval holds 66.8% of the actuals, against the 80% its normal puts
there. Corrected 2026-11-02 (review): RB-13's 80.3% is read against its
own share, not 80%. Its interval is closed on the 0.1-point lattice and
takes in the mass at both ends, so its own distribution puts 83.0% of
its mass there. RB-13 still under-covers its own 10-90% interval, by
about 3 points pooled, and is short in 6 of the 7 folds. The fitted
normal is continuous like the page's; its interval holds 84.1% against
80%. (The 83.0% is from a review re-run with RB-13's fitted (a, b)
from the JSON, a scratch script not committed; it reproduces every
fold's CRPS.) RB-13's sd averages 5.3 to 5.7 points a fold against the
page's 3.9 to 4.3. The fitted yardage spread was stable, a = 1.90 to
2.13 and b = 0.46 to 0.49.
The fitted normal's P(win) Brier (0.238082) matched RB-13's, so for
P(win) alone the wider spread is what counts.
