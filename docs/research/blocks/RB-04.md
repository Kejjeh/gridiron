# RB-04: committee or bell cow, by play-caller (pre-registered 2026-11-02)

**Status: PRE-REGISTERED.** Written before any score was seen; the only runs
before this were `--coverage-only` (coverage and the leak check, no score).

## Idea
Roadmap RB-04, "Committee or bell cow, by play-caller", from play-by-play
and the play-caller table (workstream B). Some play-callers ride one back,
others split the carries. The shipped RB set carries the back's own volume
(carries and target share over the last 3 games, snaps, expected points,
the last game) and his depth-chart rank; nothing about how the person
calling the plays distributes carries, and no history from before this
season. A linear model also cannot make "this back's role under this
play-caller" from separate columns, so the block gives the product.

## Hypothesis
Carry concentration is the play-caller's habit and follows him across
teams. Under a concentrating play-caller the lead back's share holds or
grows and the other backs' work stays small; under a committee play-caller
the lead back's share is less safe and a second back's work is real. So the
play-caller's concentration should move a back's projection up or down
depending on whether he is the lead back so far, beyond his recent volume.
Volume (rule #6).

## Features (`COLUMNS`)
| column | position | definition, as of the weeks before the row's week | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pc_rb_conc` | RB | the week's play-caller (OPC) for the back's team: per team-game he called with at least 10 back carries, the top back's share of the back carries minus the league's mean share in the same season; summed over every earlier season as play-caller (any team) and this season with this team before the row's week (against the league over this season's earlier weeks), ÷ (his games + 5) | nflverse play-by-play 2019–2025, the play-caller table |
| `rb_carry_share_std` | RB | his back carries ÷ his teams' back carries, over the games he played this season | nflverse play-by-play, snap counts, box scores |
| `rb_conc_x_share` | RB | `pc_rb_conc` × `rb_carry_share_std` | the above |

Back carry (`_tendencies.py`, `back_carries`): a designed run (`play_type`
run, `rush_attempt` 1, not a scramble, not a two-point try, not an aborted
snap) by a player who is not one of that team-game's quarterbacks (the
passer on a dropback or the runner on a scramble). The role comes from the
play (rule #4), not a position tag, so a receiver's jet sweep counts and a
quarterback's sneak does not. 2019–2025: 84,214 back carries; 3,670 of
3,741 team-games have at least 10 (the league's mean top-back share is
0.67–0.70 a season). His games are the games he was on the field for on
offense: offensive snaps > 0 in the snap counts (pfr id → gsis id through
the one crosswalk), or a box score with a carry, target or pass attempt.
The 5 games of shrinkage were set before any score (a per-game share's
noise of about 0.15 against a plausible spread of 0.06 between play-callers).

Who is in charge: as in QB-03 (the table's OPC; no row means unknown).

Missing when: `pc_rb_conc`, the team-week has no named play-caller, or he
has neither an earlier season as play-caller nor an earlier game this
season with this team; `rb_carry_share_std`, the back has no earlier game
this season, or his teams had no back carries in those games; the product,
either. Missing enters as 0 plus `<col>__known`.

Note: `rb_carry_share_std` is not in the shipped set either, so a pass
would need a follow-up to tell the interaction from the share alone. It is
here because an interaction needs both of its parts.
