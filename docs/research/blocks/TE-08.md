# TE-08: script, second-half target share trailing vs leading, by this week's spread (pre-registered 2026-11-02)

**Status: PRE-REGISTERED.** Written before any score was seen; the only runs
before this were `--coverage-only` (coverage and the leak check, no score).

## Idea
Roadmap TE-08, "Script: blocks when leading, routes when trailing", from
play-by-play. The direct measure is the tight end's routes by score state.
That cannot be built in-season: participation stops at 2025, and its routes
are about 42% filled. This block uses what play-by-play has: his share of the
team's targets while it trails and while it leads, season to date. It weights
that split by this week's pre-game spread, the expected script. The shipped
TE set has his overall target share (`tgt_share_l3`), expected points and
the team's implied total (`implied`). It has no spread (the `+line` block,
spread and weather, was rejected in FEATURE_EXPANSION.md: 4 of 7 folds) and
nothing conditional on the score.

**What it can measure:** whether a tight end's share of the targets moves
with the score after halftime, and whether this week's expected script,
applied to that split, adds anything to the shipped set.

**What it cannot measure:** routes or blocking snaps. A target share is
routes times targets per route, so a tight end kept in to block and one who
runs routes but is not thrown to look the same. The samples are small. The
split also carries his team's quarterback and coverage tendencies. The
closing line is an expectation of the script, not the script that happens.

## Hypothesis
Some tight ends block when their team leads and run routes when it trails.
Their share of the targets moves with the score, and QUANT_FOUNDATIONS §1.5
says script is a second-half effect. Such a tight end should get more
targets this week when the spread says his team will trail, and fewer when
it says his team will lead, beyond what his overall share says. The shares
are volume (rule #6), season to date because each score state is a fraction
of his games. The spread is a pre-game fact of this week.

## Features (`COLUMNS`)
| column | position | definition, as of the weeks before the row's week | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `trail_tgt_share_std` | TE | (his second-half targets with `score_differential` < 0 + 10 × his overall second-half share) ÷ (his teams' such targets + 10), over his games this season | nflverse play-by-play, snap counts, box scores |
| `lead_tgt_share_std` | TE | the same with `score_differential` > 0 | same |
| `script_tilt` | TE | (`trail_tgt_share_std` − `lead_tgt_share_std`) × the points his team is the underdog by this week (−his side of the closing `spread_line`; negative when favoured) | the above, nflverse pre-game schedule |

Definitions: second half is `qtr` ≥ 3, overtime included. `score_differential`
is his team's margin at the snap. A target is play_type `pass`,
`pass_attempt` 1, a receiver named, not a two-point try (`_p1a_pbp_roles.py`).
His overall second-half share is his second-half targets ÷ his teams'
second-half targets, any score. The pseudo-count of 10 team targets shrinks
each score-state share toward it. A team's second-half targets while leading
are under 10 for 11% of the rows, and a share of 1 from a single target is
noise that the spread would amplify. His games are the games he was on the
field for on offense: offensive snaps > 0 in the snap counts, or a box score
with a carry, target or pass attempt. His side of the line comes from the
schedule's home and away teams, mapped to today's codes. The keys' `home`
column is 0 for every 2019 Raiders row (see the team-code note in
`_p1a_pbp_roles.py`), so it is not used.

Missing when: no earlier game this season, or his teams had no second-half
targets in those games (all three). Missing enters as 0 plus `<col>__known`.

Developed with `--coverage-only` only. A first draft used raw score-state
shares (coverage 94–99%). The shrinkage was added after looking at the
denominators, not at any score.

Not shipped, not a contender.
