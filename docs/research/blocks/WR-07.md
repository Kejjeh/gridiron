# WR-07: targets per route run, last season, as a prior (pre-registered 2026-11-02)

**Status: PRE-REGISTERED.** Written before any score was seen; the only runs
before this were `--coverage-only` (coverage and the leak check, no score).
The other checks looked at the features' own distributions and at how much
of a receiver's rate is signal rather than sampling noise; none read points.

## Idea
Roadmap WR-07, "Targets per route, full coverage", from participation
routes (to 2025). The roadmap's note: free routes failed before; prior only.

**The overlap, stated plainly.** FEATURE_EXPANSION's `+routes` block already
tested targets per route as `tprr_season`. It used this season to date and
the same route proxy (dropbacks on the field), next to routes and route
share, and it failed (better on both metrics in 2 of 7 folds). The shipped
set carries target share (`tgt_share_l3`), snap share (`snap_l3`) and
expected points, which hold most of what a season-to-date rate would.

What is new here:
- **Last season's rate.** It is known from week 1, and it is the only
  version that exists live in 2026, because nflverse has no in-season
  participation.
- **That rate times his snap share this season.** The rate becomes volume
  only through playing time, and a linear model cannot form the product
  from the two on its own.

"Full coverage": the route proxy exists for every dropback (participation
`offense_players`, 100% of dropbacks).

## Hypothesis
Targets per route is the stickiest receiving rate from one season to the
next. Last season's rate, applied to this season's playing time, says what
target share his role should produce, beyond the noisy last-three-games
target share. The rate is a slow prior (rule #6); playing time is the fast
part.

## Features (`COLUMNS`)
| column | position | definition, as of the weeks before the row's week | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tprr_prior` | WR | his targets per route in season s−1, on all his teams, shrunk toward the league's rate with 60 pseudo-routes: (targets + 60·p) ÷ (routes + 60). A route is a regular-season dropback he was on the field for (participation `offense_players`), not as the play's passer; p is the league's rate that season over every receiver (any player targeted at least once on a dropback) | participation × play-by-play `receiver_player_id` |
| `tprr_prior_x_snap_l3` | WR | `tprr_prior` × his mean offensive snap share over his last 3 games this season before the week | the above × nflverse snap counts `offense_pct` (pfr id → gsis id through the crosswalk) |

**The shrinkage** is set from the rates themselves, never from points
(method of moments, receivers with 100+ routes, 2018–2025). Among wide
receivers, a between-receiver spread of τ = 0.046 is left after sampling
noise, so k = p(1 − p) ÷ τ² ≈ 64, rounded to 60. The same receiver's rate
correlates 0.65 from one season to the next (737 pairs). The wide-receiver
group for this one measurement came from box-score positions; the block
itself reads no position tag (rule #4).

Missing when: he was on the field for no dropback in s−1 (a rookie, a lost
season), which leaves both missing. Or he has no snap-count row this season
before the week, which leaves the product missing. Missing enters as 0 plus
`<col>__known`; the `tprr_prior` indicator is close to "he played last
season", which may carry signal of its own.

Real share: the route proxy is recorded on 100% of dropbacks, but it is not
a route. A receiver who stays in to block on a dropback still counts. That
is rare for wide receivers, which is why this test is WR only.
Participation's `route` column is the targeted receiver's route only (2024:
filled on 98.7% of targeted dropbacks and on 12.4% of the others), so true
routes run cannot be counted from free data. The league's rate on the proxy
is 0.165–0.171 by season (2018–2024); WR rows' `tprr_prior` averages 0.185.

Shared builders: `scripts/research/blocks/_p4_participation_priors.py`
(package P4): participation joined to regular-season play-by-play on (game,
play), so participation's playoff plays never enter.
