# WR-05: the opponent's man/zone mix against the receiver's own split (pre-registered 2026-11-02)

**Status: PRE-REGISTERED.** Written before any score was seen; the only runs
before this were `--coverage-only` (coverage and the leak check, no score).
The other checks looked at the features' own distributions and at how much
of a receiver's split is signal rather than sampling noise; none read points.

## Idea
Roadmap WR-05, "Opponent man/zone vs his splits", from participation (to
2025), prior only. The shipped WR set carries target share (`tgt_share_l3`),
air-yards share, expected points, separation (`ngs_sep`, partly a beat-man
skill) and YAC over expected, plus the opponent's WR points allowed
(`def_allowed`). None of it meets the opponent's coverage mix. No
FEATURE_EXPANSION block touched coverage.

## Hypothesis
Some receivers earn targets more often against man than against zone,
beyond the league's own gap. Such a receiver should see more targets, and so
more points, against an opponent that played more man than the league last
season. Targets per route is a target-earning rate. Here it is a split
measured last season, so it enters as a slow, shrunk prior (rule #6).

## Features (`COLUMNS`)
| column | position | definition, as of the weeks before the row's week | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `wr_man_split_prior` | WR | his targets per route against man minus against zone, minus the league's gap. Season s−1; a route is a regular-season dropback he was on the field for (participation `offense_players`), not as the play's passer, and only routes where participation records man or zone count. The league's gap is over every receiver (any player targeted at least once on a dropback that season). Empirical Bayes: the gap times h ÷ (h + 150), with h = n_man·n_zone ÷ (n_man + n_zone) his effective number of routes (0 when he has none against one of the two) | participation `offense_players`, `defense_man_zone_type` × play-by-play `receiver_player_id` |
| `opp_man_rate_prior` | WR | the opponent defense's share of its known-coverage dropbacks faced in s−1 that were man, minus the league's share that season | participation × play-by-play |
| `wr_man_matchup_prior` | WR | `wr_man_split_prior × opp_man_rate_prior` | derived |

**Why targets per route, and the shrinkage.** Measured on the inputs alone
(method of moments, wide receivers with 100+ known-coverage routes and 20+
against each side, 2018–2025):
- **Targets per route split:** a between-receiver spread of τ ≈ 0.032 is
  left after sampling noise, about a quarter of the observed spread. The
  same receiver's split correlates 0.22 from one season to the next (718
  pairs).
- **Yards per route split:** a third of that reliability, with a
  year-to-year correlation of 0.13. So the block uses targets.
- **k:** p(1 − p) ÷ τ² ≈ 150. A regular receiver's split keeps about 40% of
  its raw size.

The wide-receiver group for this one measurement came from box-score
positions; the block itself reads no position tag (rule #4). Across every
receiver the split looks far more stable (0.48 year to year), but that is
role, not skill: against the all-receiver gap, backs average −0.07 and wide
receivers +0.03. WR rows' feature averages +0.010 because of it, a constant
the main effect `opp_man_rate_prior` absorbs inside the product.

Missing when: in s−1 he had no route with man or zone recorded (a rookie, a
lost season). That makes `wr_man_split_prior` and `wr_man_matchup_prior`
missing. Missing enters as 0 plus `<col>__known`, and that indicator is
close to "he played last season", which may carry signal of its own.
`opp_man_rate_prior` is never missing.

Real share:
- **Routes are a proxy.** A "route" is a dropback with him on the field,
  recorded for 100% of dropbacks. The free data never says whether he ran a
  route or blocked. Participation's `route` column is the targeted
  receiver's route only: in 2024 it is filled on 98.7% of targeted
  dropbacks and on 12.4% of the others. Wide receivers rarely block on a
  dropback, so for them the proxy is close (FEATURE_EXPANSION's `+routes`
  block used the same one).
- **Coverage** is recorded on 88.6% (2018), 88.9% (2019), 89.3% (2020),
  89.0% (2021), 88.6% (2022), 99.6% (2023) and 99.8% (2024) of
  regular-season dropbacks. The league's man share moves with the source
  (28.8% in 2022, 48.5% in 2024), so every rate here is taken relative to
  its own season.

Shared builders: `scripts/research/blocks/_p4_participation_priors.py`
(package P4): participation joined to regular-season play-by-play on (game,
play), so participation's playoff plays never enter.
