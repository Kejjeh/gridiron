# TE-13: tight-end development curve, draft capital and age (pre-registered 2026-11-02)

**Status: PRE-REGISTERED.** Written before any score was seen. The only runs
before this were `--coverage-only` (coverage, feature counts and the leak
check, no score) and a dry run of the scoring code on random synthetic
outcomes (no real points).

## Idea
Roadmap TE-13, "Rookie tight-end development curve" (prior stats, draft
picks; kind A, phase 4, for 2027). It is tested now on 2019–2025, so the
offseason only re-tests it on 2026.

What the page does in weeks 1–3 today: the advanced model and the stack
start in week 4 (`min_week`). Before that the page serves
`gridiron.projection` on this season's box scores alone. In week 1 it
abstains for every player. In weeks 2–3 it projects a player who has an
earlier game from one or two games of usage, shrunk to role priors, and
abstains on the rest. It never reads the draft, a player's age or last
season. The shipped set (expected points, shares, depth chart) does not run
in these weeks, so the only overlap is the page's own in-season number, and
every system below carries it.

This is not a runner block: `block_runner.py` scores weeks 4–18. Its
harness is `scripts/research/early_season_backtest.py`, and the script's
docstring holds the shared method. The curve covers every tight end, not
only rookies. It is the shape over age that says how far a young tight end
outruns last season, and a rookie has no last season to outrun.

## Hypothesis
Tight ends develop late. A young tight end scores more this season than his
points per game last season say (the year-2 and year-3 jumps), and an old
one scores less. A rookie's level comes from where he was drafted, because
teams play the tight ends they invested in. Draft capital and age are fixed
facts, known before week 1. The curve's shape is age plus age squared,
centred at 27. The bar does not test signs; the per-unit coefficients of the
frozen fit are reported.

## Features
| column | position | definition, as of before the row's week | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `page` | TE | the page's number today: `gridiron.projection.project` on this season's box scores before the week, with the week's implied total; missing where it abstains | box scores, schedules (lines) |
| `ppg_prev` | TE | his league points per game over last season's regular-season games (any team); missing with fewer than 4 games | box scores |
| `te_capital` | TE | −ln(overall pick), with an undrafted player at pick 260 | nflverse draft picks |
| `te_age` | TE | his age in years on 1 September of the season | players table (`birth_date`) |
| `te_age_sq` | TE | (`te_age` − 27)² | same |

Missing when: `page` in week 1, and for a player with no earlier game this
season; `ppg_prev` with fewer than 4 games last season. The three TE-13
columns are known for every row (100%). Missing enters as 0 plus
`<col>__known`.

## Test
- Position: TE. Rows: every week 1–3 player-week of 2019–2025 with a box
  score and a Sleeper projection: 1,460 rows, 587 of them with Sleeper 5+.
- Systems, each fitted separately for weeks 1, 2 and 3 on the training
  seasons (`A.Ridge`, lam 5), with fitted predictions floored at 0:
  - `null`: the training mean.
  - `today`: the page as served, or the null where it abstains.
  - `today_fit`: [page].
  - `prior`: [page, ppg_prev], last season discounted.
  - `cand`: prior plus [te_capital, te_age, te_age_sq].

  Sleeper's projection is context only, never part of the bar.
- Seasons: 2019–2025, seven season folds; every other season trains.
- Bar (all three must hold):
  1. `cand` beats both `today` and `today_fit` on MAE and on start/sit
     pairwise (within a week, among Sleeper 5+) in every fold. This must
     hold over weeks 1–3 and again over weeks 2–3 alone.
  2. `cand` beats `prior` on MAE and pairwise in every fold (weeks 1–3):
     draft capital and age add something to last season.
  3. Out-of-fold `cand`, 2019–2025 pooled: |bias| ≤ 1.5 points in every band
     with n ≥ 10. The bands are last season's points per game (none, <5,
     5–10, 10–15, 15–20, 20+) and age (<24, 24–27, 27–30, 30+).

  `prior` is judged on conditions 1 and 3 as well, and reported as
  `prior_clears`. That is the shared core; it is not a pass for TE-13.
- Coverage seen before registering (no scores):
  - rows by season (weeks 1, 2, 3): 2019 67/70/72, 2020 74/66/68, 2021
    68/69/72, 2022 68/64/73, 2023 69/65/67, 2024 67/72/72, 2025 76/70/71
  - `page`: 2019 58%, 2020 56%, 2021 56%, 2022 54%, 2023 55%, 2024 54%, 2025 56% (by week: 0%, 80%, 87%)
  - `ppg_prev`: 2019 81%, 2020 82%, 2021 83%, 2022 83%, 2023 86%, 2024 80%, 2025 82%
  - `te_capital`, `te_age`, `te_age_sq`: 100% every season
  - band sizes (rows): last season's ppg none 258, <5 675, 5–10 424, 10–15
    95, 15–20 8 (below n = 10, not judged); age <24 245, 24–27 581, 27–30
    391, 30+ 243
  - leak check: passed (2021 week 1, 2023 week 2, 2025 week 3); data digest `622eccc82e8367f3`

## If it passes
The frozen fit (`frozen_fit` in the JSON, fitted on all seven seasons) is
applied unchanged to 2026 weeks 1–3 in the offseason (phase 4). Nothing has
looked at 2026 weeks 1–3 with it. Only if it holds there does it get a
DECISIONS.md row and a place on the 2027 week 1–3 board. Nothing changes in
2026.

**Fails.** Conditions 1 and 2 do not hold; condition 3 does.

1. TE-13 beats the page, as served and refit, on both metrics in 5 of 7
   folds. Over weeks 1–3 it beats both in all 7. Over weeks 2–3 it orders
   fewer pairs than the page as served in 2020 (54.88% vs 55.37%), and in
   2025 fewer than both (54.82% vs 56.26%), with a higher MAE than the
   refit (2.942 vs 2.907).
2. It beats the plain prior on both metrics in 3 of 7 folds (2019, 2023,
   2024; mean −0.05 points of pairwise, −0.011 MAE).
3. Every band with n ≥ 10 is within ±1.5. The largest is last season's
   10–15 points per game at −0.28 (n = 95).

The plain prior clears. It beats the page, as served and refit, on both
metrics in all 7 folds, over weeks 1–3 and over weeks 2–3, and every band
with n ≥ 10 is within ±1.5 (largest −0.44, the same 10–15 band). Over weeks
2–3 its mean gain over the refit is +2.83 points of pairwise and −0.116 MAE.
As pre-registered, that is the shared core, not a pass for TE-13.

- **Where the curve helps: week 1 only.** The page abstains there. TE-13
  beats the plain prior on both metrics in 5 of 7 folds (mean +1.48 points
  of pairwise, −0.054 MAE). Pooled over the seven test seasons, week 1 is
  60.8% of pairs and 3.00 MAE for TE-13, 59.0% and 3.06 for the prior,
  3.51 for the null. Over weeks 2–3 TE-13 orders fewer pairs than the prior
  in 6 of 7 folds (mean −0.93 points; MAE +0.011).
- **What carries it** (the frozen fit, all seven seasons, per unit). The
  draft capital: +0.99, +0.29 and +0.26 points per unit of −ln(pick) in
  weeks 1–3, which puts a 20th pick about 2.5 points above an undrafted
  tight end in week 1. Age adds nothing visible. The per-year term is
  −0.03, −0.04 and +0.07, and the squared term +0.021, −0.003 and +0.003
  per year². In week 1 the fitted curve is a shallow U, not the
  hypothesised rise and fall: against age 27 it is +0.45 at 23 and +0.23
  at 31. Last season's points per game get 0.54 in week 1; 0.47 in week 2,
  beside the page's 0.42; and 0.32 in week 3, beside the page's 0.48.
- **Sleeper** (context, not the bar) orders more pairs than TE-13 in all 7
  folds over weeks 1–3 (mean +2.41 points) and has the lower MAE in 6
  (mean −0.036); 2019 is the exception on MAE (3.647 vs 3.358). In week 1
  it is 63.0% of pairs and 2.94 MAE.
- **Post hoc, descriptive** (not part of the bar). The page as served
  over-projects tight ends in weeks 2–3 in 6 of 7 seasons (bias −0.31 to
  −1.08); 2020 is the exception (+0.36). Its own refit removes part of
  this: `today_fit`'s MAE is 0.24 lower than `today`'s over weeks 2–3
  (mean of the folds).

Draft capital gives the week-1 tight-end number a little that last season
cannot, but age adds nothing, and once the page has a game the curve
orders tight ends worse than last season alone. Not shipped, not a
contender, and so no 2026 holdout for TE-13. The plain prior's clearing
does not make it a contender either: re-testing last season, discounted,
for tight ends on 2026 would be its own pre-registration.
