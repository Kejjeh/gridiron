# RB-11: short rest, and his touches in his team's previous game when the week is short (pre-registered 2026-11-02)

**Status: PRE-REGISTERED.** Written before any score was seen; the only runs
before this were `--coverage-only` (coverage and the leak check, no score).

## Idea
Roadmap RB-11, "Workload hangover before a short week", from play-by-play
and the schedules. The shipped RB set has his last game's opportunities,
expected points and snaps (`opp_last`, `xfp_last`, `snap_last`), his
carries over the last three games and his expected points, so it knows a
back's last workload. It has nothing from the calendar: a heavy load before
four days of rest looks the same to it as before seven.

**What it can measure:** whether a back's last workload means something
different when the next game is a short week.

**What it cannot measure:** hits taken or snaps per touch, injuries that
never reach a report, or how a team manages practice. Short weeks are few
(about 7% of rows).

## Hypothesis
A back who carried a heavy load in his team's last game recovers less when
the next game comes four or five days later (a Thursday after a Sunday), so
his team may hold down his work, or he may be less effective. The effect
should grow with his last workload and appear only on a short week. Touches
are volume, read from his team's last game (fast, rule #6).

## Features (`COLUMNS`)
| column | position | definition, as of the weeks before the row's week | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `short_rest` | RB | 1 when his team has 5 or fewer days of rest (the schedule's `home_rest` or `away_rest` for its side), else 0 | nflverse pre-game schedule |
| `touches_last_x_short` | RB | his carries + receptions in his team's previous game this season (0 when he has no box score in it), times `short_rest` | schedule (the team's previous game), nflverse box scores |

Definitions (`_p5_schedule_weather.py`): his team is the team of his box
score that week (the runner's keys); its previous game is the latest week
before the row's week in which the schedule lists it. A back with no box
score in that game did not play or touched nothing: 0 touches.

Missing when: never for these rows (from week 4 on every team has an
earlier game). Nothing enters with a `__known` indicator.

Distributions seen before registering (no scores): `short_rest` is 1 on
6.8% of rows; `touches_last_x_short` is nonzero on 5.5% (maximum 33).
