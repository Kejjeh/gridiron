# K-05: forecast wind above 10 mph, precipitation and stadium elevation (pre-registered 2026-11-02)

**Status: PRE-REGISTERED.** Written before any score was seen; the only runs
before this were `--coverage-only` (coverage and the leak check, no score).

## Idea
Roadmap K-05, "Wind, precipitation, roof, altitude", from Open-Meteo and the
schedules (roof). The roof is already in the shipped K set (`dome`: 1 for a
dome or a closed roof), so it is not added again; this block adds the
weather of the outdoor games and the stadium's elevation. The shipped K set
also has the implied total and the spread (the market lowers totals for
wind), the kicker's points per game and his team's field-goal and
extra-point attempts per game.

**What it can measure:** whether wind and rain at kickoff, and thin air,
move kicker points beyond the roof and the line.

**What it cannot measure:** the forecast a lineup decision sees days before
kickoff (Open-Meteo's archived forecast is assembled from the first hours of
each model run, close to the observation), gusts, the wind's direction to
the goalposts, the wind inside a stadium bowl, or temperature (K-12's
late-season flag stands in for cold). Elevation is a handful of venues:
Denver (1,581 m) and Mexico City (2,245 m, two games) stand out; Las Vegas
(665 m) is indoors.

## Hypothesis
Wind above about 10 mph and rain shorten range and cost makes, so field
goals fall and some are not tried; thin air carries the ball farther,
indoors too. Kicker points should fall with wind and precipitation and rise
with elevation. Pre-game facts of the venue and this week's weather.

## Features (`COLUMNS`)
| column | position | definition, as of the weeks before the row's week | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `wind10` | K | the venue's wind at kickoff: the mean of the hourly 10 m wind speed at the kickoff hour and the two hours after it (mph), minus 10, floored at 0; 0 indoors (roof dome or closed) | Open-Meteo historical forecast (`gfs_seamless`), ERA5 reanalysis where the forecast has no values; nflverse pre-game schedule (kickoff, roof, stadium); `data/reference/stadiums.csv` |
| `precip3h` | K | precipitation at the venue over the game's first three hours: the sum of the three hourly totals after the kickoff hour (mm), capped at 10; 0 indoors | same |
| `elev_km` | K | the stadium's elevation (km) | `data/reference/stadiums.csv` (Copernicus DEM GLO-90 via Open-Meteo's elevation API) |

Definitions (`_p5_schedule_weather.py`): his team is his box score's team
that week. The kickoff hour is the schedule's `gameday` and `gametime`
(Eastern) in UTC, floored to the hour. The venue is the schedule's
`stadium_id`, except a neutral-site game listed at the home team's own
stadium (the six 2025 international games from week 4 on), whose venue,
elevation and weather are unknown. The 10 mph floor and the 10 mm cap are
fixed before any score, the same for every weather block of the package.

Weather data by Open-Meteo.com (CC BY 4.0). Weeks 4–18 of 2019–2025 (1,535
games): 1,045 from the archived forecast, 5 from the observed reanalysis
(the five 2019 international games, before the forecast archive covers
Europe and Mexico), 479 indoors (0 and 0), and 6 unknown (the 2025
international games listed at a home stadium); no outdoor game is left
without a value. Over the 1,050 outdoor games the forecast wind is above 10
mph in 22.6% (above 15 mph in 5.1%) and the precipitation 1 mm or more in
6.4%. Checked against the schedule's observed wind (955 outdoor games where
it has one): correlation 0.73, means 7.4 and 8.6 mph.

Missing when: the venue is unknown (all three). Missing enters as 0 plus
`<col>__known`.

Distributions seen before registering (no scores): `wind10` is nonzero on
15.3% of K rows (mean 0.52, maximum 20.2); `precip3h` on 6.0% (95th
percentile 0.4 mm; the 10 mm cap binds in a few games); `elev_km` mean 0.19,
maximum 2.245.

Wind, rain and altitude lift the kicker ordering in six folds, by 73 to 109 pairs (of 5,602 to 5,764) in 2022–2024, but 2025 loses 29 pairs and 0.014 of error and 2019's error is higher: a real-looking, unsteady signal the every-fold bar does not admit.
