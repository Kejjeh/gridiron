# QB-04: play-caller's pace and no-huddle rate (pre-registered 2026-11-02)

**Status: PRE-REGISTERED.** Written before any score was seen; the only runs
before this were `--coverage-only` (coverage and the leak check, no score).

## Idea
Roadmap QB-04, "Pace and no-huddle by play-caller", from play-by-play and the
play-caller table (workstream B). The `+team` block's offensive plays per
game over the last 3 games was a near miss (4 of 7 folds,
FEATURE_EXPANSION.md). A team's play count mixes tempo with how long its
drives last and how often it trails. This block measures the tempo of the
PERSON calling the plays, in neutral situations, with his history from
before this season. The shipped QB set carries the quarterback's own volume
this season and the implied team total, nothing about tempo.

## Hypothesis
Tempo is a play-caller's choice: how fast his offense gets to the line when
the clock is running and how often it skips the huddle. A faster play-caller
runs more plays, so his quarterback gets more dropbacks than three games of
volume say. The habit follows the person and is stable, so his career plus
this season estimates it better than the team's last 3 games. Volume (rule
#6).

## Features (`COLUMNS`)
| column | position | definition, as of the weeks before the row's week | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pc_pace` | QB | the week's play-caller (OPC) for the QB's team: seconds of game clock between consecutive snaps when the clock ran, minus the league's mean in the same season, summed over his snap pairs in every earlier season as play-caller (any team) and this season with this team before the row's week (against the league's mean over this season's earlier weeks), ÷ (his pairs + 50). Negative is faster than the league. | nflverse play-by-play 2019–2025, the play-caller table |
| `pc_no_huddle` | QB | the same construction for his neutral plays' `no_huddle` flag (rate over the league's), ÷ (his plays + 100) | same |

Snap pair (`_tendencies.py`, `pace_obs`): two consecutive play-by-play rows
of one game (ordered by `order_sequence`), both scrimmage plays (`play_type`
pass or run, not a two-point try, not an aborted snap) in the same drive
and quarter, where the earlier play left the clock running (not an
incompletion, not out of bounds, no penalty flag) and the later play is
neutral; the value is the game-clock seconds between the snaps, kept when
0 < seconds ≤ 60. A timeout or any other row between them breaks the pair.
A team-season has 198–349 such pairs (mean 271); the league's mean is 38.0
to 38.7 seconds. Neutral play: 0.2 ≤ wp ≤ 0.8, quarters 1–3, not the last
two minutes of the first half. The shrinkage (50 pairs, 100 plays) was set
before any score from the per-observation noise (about 8 seconds a pair; a
0/1 flag) and a plausible spread between play-callers.

Who is in charge: as in QB-03 (the table's OPC for that team-week; no row
means unknown). 3,001 of 3,070 team-games of weeks 4–18, 2019–2025 have a
named play-caller.

Missing when: the team-week has no named play-caller, or he has neither an
earlier season as play-caller nor an earlier game this season with this
team (his first week in charge, with no career in the table). Missing
enters as 0 plus `<col>__known`.
