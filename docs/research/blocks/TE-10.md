# TE-10: touchdown-driven outcome spread, tight ends (pre-registered 2026-11-02)

**Status: PRE-REGISTERED.** Written before any score was seen. The only runs
before this were `--coverage-only` (coverage and the leak check, no score)
and a plumbing test of the harness in which every real outcome was replaced
by a random draw before anything was fitted, so no real score exists.

## Idea
Roadmap TE-10, "TD-driven outcome spread" (kind D, workstream D): RB-13's
distribution for tight ends, in the same code (`blocks/_p7_distributions.py`)
with the position changed. The page gives every tight end one normal: the
served mean and the baseline's sd, max(0.65 × baseline, 1.5)
(`gridiron.projection.WEEKLY_CV`, a literature value, not a fit). That normal
is the ± the page prints and what its P(win) sums. The shipped set already
carries the MEAN (every tight-end feature block scored so far, eight of
them, failed against it); this block does not touch the mean. It changes the shape: a tight end's week is a few catches,
often one touchdown or none.

## Hypothesis
A tight end's points are more touchdown-dependent than a receiver's, so his
week is lumpier: a Poisson count of touchdowns plus a right-skewed yardage
part should describe it better than one symmetric normal, most of all for
the red-zone tight end whose share of expected points from touchdowns is
high. The share is volume (expected touchdowns price his targets by field
position), so it updates fast; no efficiency enters (rule #6).

## Distribution inputs (`COLUMNS`)
| column | position | definition, as of the weeks before the row's week | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `td_share_std` | TE | Σ expected touchdown points ÷ Σ expected fantasy points over his games this season. Each game's expected stats (`rush_touchdown_exp`, `rec_touchdown_exp`, `rush_yards_gained_exp`, `rec_yards_gained_exp`, `receptions_exp`, the two-point `_exp` columns) are scored with the league's weights, read from `gridiron.scoring.fantasy_points` through unit stat lines (rule #2) | nflverse `ff_opportunity` (weekly), regular-season games only |
| `td_games_std` | TE | how many such games | same |
| `td_share_prior` | TE | his last-season share, shrunk to the position's league share last season with weight g / (g + 3), g = his games last season | same, season − 1 |

Missing when: no earlier game this season (`td_share_std`, `td_games_std`).
The distribution then uses the prior alone (weight 0 on the season to date).
The prior is never missing. These are distribution inputs, not ridge
features, so no `__known` indicator enters anything.

## Distribution (`pmf`, `fit`; shared code `blocks/_p7_distributions.py`)
For a row with the page's mean μ:
- share s = w · `td_share_std` + (1 − w) · `td_share_prior`, w = g / (g + 3),
  g = `td_games_std` (w = 0 when missing), clipped to [0, 0.95];
- touchdowns N ~ Poisson(s · μ / 6), 6 being the league's rushing and
  receiving touchdown value (asserted equal);
- the rest (yards, catches, two-point tries, fumbles) Y ~ gamma with mean
  (1 − s) · μ and sd a + b · (1 − s) · μ;
- points = 6N + Y on the 0.1-point lattice (this league's points are
  multiples of 0.1), 0 to 90 points, the tail folded into the last bin, then
  moved so the mean is exactly μ with every point still on the 0.1 grid: by
  whole tenths, and the remaining fraction of a tenth as a one-tenth
  Bernoulli step (`on_grid_mean`; for an outcome on the grid this is never
  worse in CRPS than sliding the support off it). The move is under 0.05
  points. The harness refuses a run in which any row's mean differs from
  the page's by more than 1e-6.
- Known limits, kept on purpose: the lattice starts at 0, so a negative week
  (a lost fumble: 0.3% of the TE rows) always sits below the distribution;
  touchdowns and yardage are independent given μ.
- (a, b): one pair per fold, fitted on the six training seasons by minimum
  mean CRPS (Nelder-Mead from (1.0, 0.4), a in [0.05, 8], b in [0, 1.5]).
- μ is the harness's: the shipped ridge refit with the row's season held
  out; the training rows' μ come from models fit on the five seasons other
  than the test season and their own, so (a, b) are fitted on
  out-of-sample means like the ones they are scored on.

## Test
- Position: TE.
- Harness: `scripts/research/distribution_backtest.py` (block_runner.py's
  tables, rows and folds; pwin_calibration.py's leagues; neither changed).
- Rows: block_runner.py's TE rows (weeks 4–18, players Sleeper projected),
  2019–2025, seven season folds, every other season training.
- Comparator: the page's normal, N(μ, max(0.65 × baseline, 1.5)).
- Bar: a lower mean CRPS than the page's normal in every one of the seven
  folds, AND the pooled P(win) Brier over the seven folds' synthetic leagues
  not worse (≤), both decided on unrounded numbers. Leagues:
  pwin_calibration.py's 12-team leagues (its dealing,
  seeds and slot pools, checked against its own `week_matchups`), dealt
  from the fold's rows and means, weeks 4–17, 32 seeded leagues a week. Every
  starter keeps the page's normal except the tight ends (the TE slot and any
  tight end dealt to FLEX), whose distributions are this one; P(win) is
  computed exactly on the lattice.
- Reported, never the bar: pinball loss at 10/50/90%, the share of actuals
  inside the 10–90% interval, log loss, a week-clustered bootstrap of the
  Brier change, and a fitted normal N(μ, a + bμ) fitted the same way on the
  same rows (is a gain the shape, or only a refitted spread?).
- Coverage seen before registering (no scores):
  - `td_share_std`: 2019 97%, 2020 96%, 2021 96%, 2022 99%, 2023 98%, 2024 97%, 2025 96%
  - `td_games_std`: the same
  - `td_share_prior`: 100% every season
  - leak check: passed (2021 week 9, 2024 week 14)

**PASS** on the pre-registered bar. CRPS is lower than the page's normal
in all 7 folds, by 0.094 to 0.152 points (mean -0.124; pooled 2.152
against 2.276, 5.4% lower). The pooled P(win) Brier is not worse:
0.238099 against 0.238118 over 18,811 matchups, -0.0000192 unrounded
(week-clustered 95% interval -0.0000405 to +0.0000026; 96% of the
bootstrap draws not worse). It is lower in five folds and higher in two
(2024, 2025).

Most of the gain is the shape, not only a refitted spread: TE-10 is below
the fitted normal in every fold (pooled 2.228), and beats the page on
pinball loss at 10%, 50% and 90% in every fold (pooled 0.449 / 1.495 /
0.943 against 0.512 / 1.568 / 0.989). The median is where a normal cannot
follow: its median is its mean, while a tight end's week is skewed, and
TE-10's lower median scores better. The page's spread is too narrow for
tight ends: its 10-90% interval holds 69.3% of the actuals (80% stated),
TE-10's 77.2%, the fitted normal's 85.4%; TE-10's sd averages 3.9 to 4.3
points a fold against the page's 2.9 to 3.2. The fitted yardage spread
was stable, a = 1.05 to 1.13 and b = 0.59 to 0.60 over the seven folds.
The fitted normal's P(win) Brier (0.238098) matched TE-10's, so for
P(win) alone the wider spread is what counts.
